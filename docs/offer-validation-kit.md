# Harkingbade Offer Validation Kit

Status: ready for founder and buyer validation  
Updated: 2026-08-16

## Purpose

Turn four plausible service concepts into evidence-backed offers without publishing invented demand, arbitrary scarcity, or unsupported pricing.

The first commercial objective is not maximum revenue. It is one qualified problem, one credible proposal, one paid pilot, and enough delivery evidence to decide what to repeat.

## Offer order

1. **Product & Growth Diagnostic** — lowest delivery risk and the best first paid validation.
2. **Position-to-Launch Sprint** — sell after customer-language and launch artifacts can be demonstrated.
3. **Conversion & Growth Sprint** — sell only where a usable baseline and implementation access exist.
4. **Fractional Product & Growth Builder** — offer after repeated work proves ongoing ownership is the actual need.

Do not sell all four equally. Lead with the Diagnostic until buyer evidence identifies a stronger wedge.

## Qualification scorecard

Score each factor `0`, `1`, or `2` before proposing work.

| Factor | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Problem consequence | Cosmetic or vague | Material but unquantified | Material and tied to a decision or outcome |
| Urgency | No timing reason | Desired this quarter | A real decision, launch, or loss window exists |
| Evidence access | No access | Partial access | Customer, product, commercial, and journey evidence available |
| Decision authority | Unknown | Influencer involved | Accountable decision-maker participating |
| Implementation path | Nobody owns change | Ownership uncertain | Named team/person can implement or approve |
| Working fit | Task order only | Some strategic access | Open to diagnosis, tradeoffs, and evidence-based change |

Interpretation:

- `9–12`: qualified for a scoped conversation.
- `6–8`: discovery only; resolve gaps before a proposal.
- `0–5`: decline, refer, or offer a self-serve resource.

Any legal, safety, ethical, credential, data-access, or unrealistic-results conflict overrides the score.

## Buyer interview script

Use eight to twelve conversations in one chosen buyer segment. Do not mix unrelated audiences and call the pattern validated.

1. What consequential product, offer, launch, or growth decision is currently difficult?
2. What triggered attention now?
3. What has already been tried, and what happened?
4. Which evidence is trusted, missing, or disputed?
5. Where do handoffs between product, marketing, content, design, engineering, ecommerce, or analytics break?
6. What happens if this remains unresolved for 90 days?
7. Who owns the decision and who must implement it?
8. What would a useful intervention produce that the team does not have today?
9. What alternatives are being considered—including doing nothing or hiring?
10. How are external projects bought, approved, and paid for?

Record exact language, consequence, urgency, evidence, authority, alternatives, procurement, and objections. Do not convert compliments into demand evidence.

## Pricing validation

Pricing must satisfy both a delivery floor and a buyer-value test.

### Delivery floor

Calculate privately for each offer:

```text
minimum viable fee =
  (estimated delivery days × minimum sustainable day value)
  + direct delivery costs
  + acquisition/admin allowance
  + uncertainty reserve
```

Include research, preparation, meetings, revisions, implementation, handoff, and measurement—not only visible production time.

### Buyer-value test

Ask what decision delay, rework, lost conversion, failed launch, missed pipeline, or additional hiring would cost. Price should remain a credible fraction of the consequence while never promising a result outside Harkingbade's control.

### Validation sequence

1. Choose one buyer segment and one billing currency.
2. Calculate the internal floor and walk-away conditions.
3. Test a single transparent range in three qualified conversations.
4. Record the reaction: affordable, requires proof, requires procurement, too broad, too narrow, or not valuable.
5. Change scope before discounting the same workload.
6. Publish a range only after at least three comparable proposals and one paid delivery establish a defensible pattern.

## Proposal skeleton

Every proposal should fit on a small number of pages and contain:

1. **Situation:** the buyer's context in their language.
2. **Decision:** the question the engagement must answer.
3. **Evidence:** what will be reviewed and what is unavailable.
4. **Outcome:** the decision/artifact expected—not a guaranteed commercial result.
5. **Scope:** activities, collaborators, cadence, and revision boundary.
6. **Exclusions:** what is explicitly outside the engagement.
7. **Dependencies:** access, response times, decision-maker participation, implementation ownership.
8. **Measure:** baseline, observation window, threshold, and follow-up decision.
9. **Commercials:** fee, currency, taxes, payment timing, expiry, and change control.
10. **Evidence permission:** what may later be described publicly, anonymized, or kept private.

## First 30-day commercial sprint

### Week 1 — choose and prepare

- Select one buyer segment and one role/problem wedge.
- Complete the founder evidence intake for three relevant projects.
- Turn the Diagnostic into a sample decision brief using Harkingbade itself.
- Build a list of twenty-five relevant people from existing relationships and public professional context; do not scrape or mass-spam.

### Week 2 — learn

- Request eight problem interviews.
- Run at least four.
- Record language, consequence, urgency, authority, and buying process.
- Revise the service page only when a repeated pattern appears.

### Week 3 — propose

- Invite qualified interviewees to one bounded paid Diagnostic.
- Send no more than three comparable proposals.
- Record every objection and non-decision.

### Week 4 — decide

- If one pilot sells, deliver with an evidence ledger and request outcome/proof permission.
- If conversations occur but proposals fail, revise proof, scope, or price.
- If conversations do not occur, revise audience and outreach route before changing the offer.
- If the problem is not consequential, retire the wedge and test the next one.
