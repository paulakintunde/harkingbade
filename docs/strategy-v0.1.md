# Harkingbade Revival Strategy v0.1

Status: superseded by `docs/repositioning-v1.md`; retained as the initial research record  
Date: 2026-08-16

## Executive decision

Rebuild Harkingbade as a proof-led personal brand for a **product and growth builder**: someone who can take an opportunity from customer insight through positioning, product/website delivery, launch, and measurable growth.

Do not rebuild it as a general tech-news or tutorial blog. Do not lead with a list of unrelated skills. Product management, marketing, branding, content, social, web development, and ecommerce are the capabilities behind one commercial outcome: **turning ideas into useful products and products into demand**.

The website must do four jobs in this order:

1. Win trust and qualified career opportunities.
2. Generate leads for productized services and fractional work.
3. Build an owned email audience around original field-tested knowledge.
4. Sell products and, only after meaningful traffic returns, monetize selected editorial pages with ads.

There is no honest “undisputed” or guaranteed traffic strategy. The defensible strategy is a staged system with explicit evidence gates, downside controls, and multiple revenue paths so the business does not depend on Google traffic alone.

## What the initial audit found

- The workspace is blank; this is a clean Astro rebuild rather than an in-place migration.
- Public third-party checks reported `harkingbade.com` offline with no resolved server IP in May 2026.
- Google still exposes a very small sample of old URLs, including a 2017 Angular IDE article whose displayed modified date was changed to 2025 without bringing the underlying recommendations up to date.
- The Internet Archive exposes roughly 319 non-taxonomy legacy paths. The footprint is extremely broad: Windows and phone fixes, downloads, games and cheats, login-intent pages, SEO, programming, careers/productivity, giveaways, and a new VPS cluster.
- The 2025 VPS cluster reads as generic and repetitive rather than demonstrably first-hand. The archived home page also used generic “news and expert insights” positioning that did not match a focused personal brand.
- Some legacy paths appear risky or obsolete: software activation, emulators, jailbreaks, movie/download intent, old operating systems and devices, login-intent pages, and dated price/release content.

These facts suggest at least two distinct causes of the current zero-traffic state:

1. **Immediate availability failure:** an offline or non-resolving domain cannot sustain search traffic.
2. **Underlying quality and focus failure:** broad search-arbitrage topics, stale tutorials, generic new content, and weak authorship make the site vulnerable even after uptime is restored.

The exact historical traffic-loss cause cannot be declared until Google Search Console, GA/GA4, backlink, DNS, security, and manual-action data are reviewed. Google’s own traffic-drop guide identifies technical problems, algorithmic changes, security/spam issues, demand shifts, and migrations as separate causes that require different remedies: <https://developers.google.com/search/docs/monitor-debug/debugging-search-traffic-drops>.

## Positioning

### Recommended category

**Product & Growth Builder**

This is intentionally broader than “product manager” but much sharper than “digital expert.” It supports roles such as growth product manager, product marketing manager, digital product manager, growth strategist, ecommerce/product lead, and zero-to-one operator while also supporting consulting and product sales.

### Working promise

> I turn customer insight into products, brands, and growth systems that people choose.

Alternative, more technical version:

> I connect product strategy, brand, and hands-on delivery to take digital ideas from zero to measurable growth.

### Brand point of view

The differentiator should not be “I can do many things.” It should be:

> Growth breaks when strategy, message, product, and execution live in separate silos. I work across the seams and make the system cohere.

That point of view is credible only when the site shows artifacts, decisions, experiments, and outcomes.

### Audience priority

1. **Hiring leaders** seeking a cross-functional product/growth operator.
2. **Founder-led and lean teams** that need research, positioning, launch, conversion, or ecommerce help without hiring a full department.
3. **Ambitious multidisciplinary builders** who can become readers and buyers of tools, templates, and education.

The home page can route these audiences without presenting three different brands: `See my work`, `Work with me`, and `Learn with me`.

## Proof architecture

The new brand must be built on evidence, not self-description.

### Required proof objects

- Three to five case studies using problem → constraints → decisions → work → measurable result → reflection.
- A capabilities map that connects each skill to business outcomes, not a wall of badges.
- A “now” or lab page with active experiments and changelogs.
- Original teardown articles using screenshots, data, annotated flows, or working prototypes.
- Testimonials or attributable references where permission exists.
- A transparent bio, real identity, location/market served, and verifiable social/profile links.
- Clear authorship and editorial/update notes on every article.

### Case-study candidates

- The Harkingbade recovery itself: archive audit, content pruning, Astro migration, Cloudflare deployment, SEO recovery, and revenue experiments.
- An ecommerce funnel or store improvement with before/after metrics.
- A product discovery or launch case with customer evidence and decision tradeoffs.
- A brand/positioning project tied to conversion or demand rather than visual identity alone.
- A content/social system tied to pipeline, subscribers, qualified reach, or revenue.

The rebuild should be public proof of the combined product, marketing, SEO, development, and operating skill set.

## Revenue system

Ads are a late-stage layer, not the business model. The fastest plausible revenue comes from selling high-value expertise; scalable revenue is added only after proof and distribution exist.

### Revenue ladder

| Stage | Offer | Purpose | Evidence gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Paid diagnostic/audit | First revenue and customer language | A repeatable audit rubric and sample output |
| 2 | Fixed-scope sprint | Solve one valuable problem with a clear boundary | Two strong case studies or references |
| 3 | Fractional product/growth engagement | Higher-value recurring income | Reliable delivery system and clear capacity |
| 4 | Templates, playbooks, calculators, mini-tools | Scalable entry product and lead qualification | Repeated audience problem observed in calls/content |
| 5 | Workshop or cohort | Monetize teaching and community | Engaged list and validated learning outcome |
| 6 | Sponsorship, affiliate, and selective ads | Monetize qualified attention | Stable brand-safe traffic and editorial controls |

### Provisional offer menu

- **Product & Growth Diagnostic:** research, funnel, positioning, analytics, and priority map.
- **Position-to-Launch Sprint:** customer insight, value proposition, landing experience, measurement plan, and launch assets.
- **Conversion System Sprint:** offer, journey, messaging, page, analytics, and experiment backlog.
- **Fractional Product/Growth Builder:** ongoing cross-functional ownership for a lean team.

Pricing should be set only after market, geography, proof, delivery capacity, and target-client interviews are known. The site should publish scope and outcomes before it publishes price.

### Advertising gates

- AdSense requires original, high-quality content that attracts an audience: <https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/9724>.
- Journey by Mediavine currently states that entry starts above 1,000 sessions and evaluates original audience-first content and human readers: <https://help.mediavine.com/what-does-it-take-to-get-approved-by-mediavine>.
- Raptive currently requires at least 25,000 monthly pageviews plus original content, meaningful human involvement, long-form content, analytics, domain age, and traffic-market requirements: <https://help.raptive.com/hc/en-us/articles/360032840891-Who-is-eligible-for-Raptive>.

These are milestones, not the first objective. A small number of qualified consulting leads can be worth more than large volumes of low-intent pageviews.

## Content moat

### Three editorial pillars

1. **Product & Growth Systems** — discovery, prioritization, experiments, analytics, go-to-market, product marketing.
2. **Brand to Demand** — positioning, customer language, content systems, social distribution, SEO, conversion.
3. **Build & Commerce** — Astro/Cloudflare, lightweight product delivery, ecommerce systems, automation, and real implementation notes.

Every article must connect to the central promise and demonstrate experience. A generic post that could be published under any author should not be published.

### Content formats

- Field notes from live work.
- Annotated teardowns.
- Original experiments with baseline, intervention, and result.
- Decision frameworks with downloadable tools.
- Case studies and postmortems.
- Comparison/review content only when products were actually used and the evaluation method is shown.

Google explicitly warns against publishing many unrelated topics for search traffic, mass automation without added value, shallow summaries, and fake freshness. It asks for a clear site purpose, first-hand expertise, authorship, and original analysis: <https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content>. Its current spam policy also treats large volumes of low-value generated pages as scaled-content abuse: <https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies>.

### Publishing operating model

- One flagship evidence-rich article every two weeks.
- One smaller field note or teardown each week.
- Repurpose each flagship into LinkedIn/social posts, an email issue, a short video, and one reusable visual.
- Run one quarterly original research or benchmark project designed to earn citations and links.
- Update only when the substance changes; retain original publication and meaningful modification dates.

## SEO recovery plan

### Phase 0 — preserve evidence before changing URLs

Obtain and export:

- Google Search Console performance by query, page, country, device, and search type for the longest available period.
- Page indexing, crawl statistics, security issues, manual actions, sitemaps, removals, and link reports.
- GA/GA4 landing-page and channel history.
- Existing WordPress/database/media backup if available.
- Current registrar, DNS, Cloudflare zone, certificate, and hosting state.
- Backlink exports from at least one reputable index.
- Historical top landing pages and historical revenue by page/category if available.

### Phase 1 — restore a trustworthy technical baseline

- Restore domain resolution and HTTPS.
- Select one canonical host, likely `https://harkingbade.com`, and permanently redirect protocol/host variants.
- Launch a fast, complete minimum site with home, about, work/case studies, services, insights, contact, privacy, terms, and a true 404.
- Verify Search Console domain ownership, submit sitemaps, inspect representative URLs, and monitor server/crawl errors.
- Keep staging and `workers.dev` preview hosts out of the index.

### Phase 2 — classify every legacy URL

Each old URL gets exactly one disposition:

1. **Keep and substantially improve** when it still satisfies the same user need, is safe, and can demonstrate real expertise.
2. **Consolidate and 301** only to a page that genuinely satisfies the same intent.
3. **410/404** when obsolete, risky, unsupported, or without a relevant replacement.
4. **Legal/security review** for activation, piracy/download, privacy-invasive, or misleading-intent pages.

Do not redirect hundreds of unrelated posts to the home page. Google advises relevant URL mapping and warns that mass irrelevant redirects may be treated as soft 404s: <https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/site-move-with-url-changes>. Google also states that removed pages without close replacements should return 404 or 410: <https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/troubleshoot-crawling-errors>.

### Phase 3 — rebuild topical authority

- Start with one commercial topic cluster that matches the chosen offer and strongest proof.
- Create one definitive pillar, supporting problem/decision pages, one tool/template, and at least one case study.
- Add internal links based on user journeys, not keyword stuffing.
- Earn links through original assets, expert contributions, partnerships, and useful tools—not bulk outreach or guest-post schemes.
- Expand to a second cluster only after the first produces qualified impressions, subscribers, leads, or citations.

### Measurement tree

North-star measure: **qualified opportunities created by owned expertise**.

Leading indicators:

- Search: valid indexed pages, non-brand impressions, query diversity, qualified organic visits.
- Trust: case-study completion, repeat visits, direct traffic, branded search, citations/backlinks.
- Audience: email conversion rate, engaged subscribers, replies, return readership.
- Career: portfolio views, résumé downloads, recruiter/hiring-manager inquiries, interviews influenced.
- Business: qualified leads, calls, proposals, close rate, revenue, revenue per 1,000 sessions.
- Reliability: uptime, crawl errors, Core Web Vitals, Worker/API errors, form delivery.

Traffic alone is not the primary success measure.

## Astro and Cloudflare architecture

### Decision

Use a **static-first Astro site deployed as Cloudflare Workers Static Assets**, with a small Worker only for paths that need runtime behavior.

- Pre-render all public marketing, case-study, article, and resource pages.
- Use Astro content collections with a strict schema for titles, summaries, canonical paths, publication/update dates, authorship, content pillar, evidence type, draft state, images, redirects, and SEO controls.
- Use the Worker for `/api/*` endpoints such as contact/newsletter submission, rate limiting, bot protection, and future authenticated tools.
- Keep asset-first routing for public pages; selectively run Worker code first only where needed.
- Use a `_redirects` file for the reviewed legacy 301 map. Workers Static Assets supports up to 2,000 static redirects, enough for the discovered legacy footprint: <https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/static-assets/redirects/>.
- Use a custom `404.html` with a real 404 status via `not_found_handling: "404-page"`.
- Use `_headers` for security headers, canonical preview behavior, and long-lived caching of hashed assets.
- Set the current compatibility date at implementation time, enable only required compatibility flags, generate binding types with Wrangler, keep secrets out of source, and enable logs/traces before production. Current guidance: <https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/best-practices/workers-best-practices/>.

Cloudflare recommends Workers for new Astro projects, and Astro pre-renders pages by default; the Cloudflare adapter is only required for on-demand rendering: <https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/framework-guides/web-apps/astro/> and <https://docs.astro.build/en/guides/deploy/cloudflare/>.

### SEO implementation requirements

- Canonical URL and unique title/description rules.
- `sitemap-index.xml`, `robots.txt`, RSS, web manifest, and crawlable HTML navigation.
- `ProfilePage` + `Person` on About, `Article` on editorial pages, `BreadcrumbList` where visible, and appropriate product/service markup only when page content supports it.
- Open Graph and social cards generated from controlled templates.
- Author entity with consistent `sameAs` references and real credentials.
- Image dimensions, responsive formats, lazy loading below the fold, and no layout shift.
- Minimal client JavaScript; interactive islands only where they improve the task.
- Accessible semantics, keyboard operation, focus, contrast, reduced-motion support, and validated forms.
- Search Console, GA4 or a privacy-suitable product analytics layer, Cloudflare Web Analytics, and consent handling appropriate to the markets served.
- Event taxonomy defined before launch: CTA, case study, email, resource, contact, and application events.

## 90-day recovery sequence

### Days 1–14: evidence and uptime

- Recover domain/DNS/Cloudflare access and restore a secure response.
- Export analytics and Search Console data before it ages out.
- Recover content/database/media backups and build the complete URL ledger.
- Conduct a founder proof inventory and choose the first target role/client segment.
- Finalize positioning and the first productized offer.

### Days 15–35: minimum authority site

- Build the Astro/Workers foundation and design system.
- Publish home, about, work, services, insights index, contact, newsletter, legal pages, and 404.
- Publish at least two strong case studies and one flagship point-of-view article.
- Implement canonicalization, sitemaps, structured data, analytics, conversion events, and the first reviewed redirect batch.

### Days 36–60: demand engine

- Publish the first topic cluster and a useful lead magnet/tool.
- Start a consistent newsletter and one primary social distribution channel.
- Run ten audience/customer conversations and use exact language to refine offers and content.
- Conduct targeted proof-first outreach to employers/clients with relevant teardowns or artifacts.

### Days 61–90: validate revenue

- Sell and deliver the diagnostic or sprint to early qualified buyers.
- Convert delivery artifacts into another case study and reusable product.
- Review query/page performance, email engagement, lead quality, and conversion paths.
- Expand only the content and offers with evidence of demand.
- Apply for ad monetization only if the editorial experience and traffic quality justify it.

## Decisions still requiring founder evidence

The next strategy version needs:

1. Real name/public identity and preferred professional title.
2. Target geography and whether pricing will be in CAD, USD, or another currency.
3. Top three role types and top three employer/company types.
4. Best completed projects, measurable outcomes, artifacts, testimonials, and confidentiality constraints.
5. Strongest and weakest capabilities, plus work the founder does not want to sell.
6. Existing résumé, LinkedIn/profile URLs, social accounts, mailing list, and brand assets.
7. Registrar, DNS, Cloudflare, Search Console, Analytics, AdSense, WordPress, and email-platform access status.
8. Historical traffic peak dates, drop date/shape, top pages, and any manual, security, or policy notifications.
9. Weekly time available and cash budget for tools, design, content, and distribution.
10. Preferred balance among job search, consulting, products, and publishing for the next six months.

## Immediate no-go rules

- No mass AI publishing.
- No fake date refreshes.
- No blanket redirects to the home page.
- No restoration of risky download/activation/cheat/login-intent pages without explicit legal, safety, and relevance review.
- No claim of expertise without visible proof.
- No attempt to target all listed skills equally in navigation or the hero.
- No dependence on ads or Google as the sole revenue/distribution channel.
- No full-site SSR when static generation satisfies the page.
